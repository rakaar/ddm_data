# Results: 2026-08-05

## Replacing a point evidence delay with a delay distribution

### Short answer

Yes. If the evidence delay \(D\) is independent of the proactive process, the
proactive density \(p_A\) and CDF \(c_A\) remain unchanged. The evidence density
and CDF are averaged over the delay distribution. Equivalently, the complete
fixed-delay likelihood is averaged over \(D\).

The familiar choice-collapsed proactive-plus-reactive RT density for a fixed
delay \(d\) is

\[
p_T(t\mid d)
=
p_A(t)\left[1-c_E(t\mid d)\right]
+
p_E(t\mid d)\left[1-c_A(t)\right].
\]

For \(D\sim q(d)\), define

\[
\bar p_E(t)
=
\int q(d)\,p_E(t\mid d)\,\mathrm{d}d,
\qquad
\bar c_E(t)
=
\int q(d)\,c_E(t\mid d)\,\mathrm{d}d.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
p_T(t)
=
p_A(t)\left[1-\bar c_E(t)\right]
+
\bar p_E(t)\left[1-c_A(t)\right]
}
\]

Thus, the proposed replacement is correct: \(p_E\) and \(c_E\) become their
delay-averaged versions, while \(p_A\) and \(c_A\) do not change.

### Why this is exact

The distributed-delay likelihood is the mixture

\[
p_T(t)=\int q(d)\,p_T(t\mid d)\,\mathrm{d}d.
\]

Substituting the fixed-delay race likelihood gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
p_T(t)
&=
\int q(d)
\left\{
p_A(t)[1-c_E(t\mid d)]
+p_E(t\mid d)[1-c_A(t)]
\right\}\mathrm{d}d \\
&=
p_A(t)[1-\bar c_E(t)]
+\bar p_E(t)[1-c_A(t)],
\end{aligned}
\]

because the proactive terms do not depend on \(d\) and can be taken outside
the integral.

### Uniform delay gives useful analytic identities

Let

\[
D\sim\operatorname{Uniform}(a,b),
\qquad
x=t-t_{\mathrm{stim}},
\]

and let \(p_E^0(u)\) and \(c_E^0(u)\) denote the reactive density and CDF as
functions of elapsed evidence time, before applying the afferent delay. Both are
zero for \(u\leq 0\). Then

\[
p_E(t\mid d)=p_E^0(x-d),
\qquad
c_E(t\mid d)=c_E^0(x-d).
\]

The delay-averaged evidence density is

\[
\boxed{
\bar p_E(t)
=
\frac{c_E^0(x-a)-c_E^0(x-b)}{b-a}
}
\]

because integrating a density over delay produces a difference of CDF values.

For the delay-averaged evidence CDF, define the CDF antiderivative

\[
H_E(z)=
\begin{cases}
\displaystyle\int_0^z c_E^0(u)\,\mathrm{d}u, & z>0,\\
0, & z\leq 0.
\end{cases}
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
\bar c_E(t)
=
\frac{H_E(x-a)-H_E(x-b)}{b-a}
}
\]

The same identities apply separately to the upper- and lower-bound evidence
densities and CDFs.

### Full RT-plus-choice likelihood

The implemented likelihood retains the chosen bound and the
\(\delta_{\mathrm{go}}\) grace interval. Let \(s\in\{+,-\}\) be the observed
choice, and define

\[
\tau_d=t-t_{\mathrm{stim}}-d,
\qquad
\tau_d^+=\tau_d+\delta_{\mathrm{go}}.
\]

For a fixed delay, the current likelihood has the form

\[
\begin{aligned}
L_s(t\mid d)
={}&p_A(t)
\left[
\frac{1}{2}\left(1-c_E(\tau_d^+)\right)
+c_{E,s}(\tau_d^+)-c_{E,s}(\tau_d)
\right] \\
&+p_{E,s}(\tau_d)\left[1-c_A(t)\right],
\end{aligned}
\]

where \(c_E=c_{E,+}+c_{E,-}\). Therefore, for RT-plus-choice fitting, every
evidence term evaluated at either \(\tau_d\) or \(\tau_d^+\) must be averaged
over the same delay distribution:

\[
L_s(t)=\int q(d)\,L_s(t\mid d)\,\mathrm{d}d.
\]

The proactive terms still remain unchanged. When \(L_+(t)\) and \(L_-(t)\)
are summed, the grace-interval terms cancel and the compact choice-collapsed
equation above is recovered.

### RT-window normalization must also change

If fitting only RTs in a window \([L,U]\), the normalization cannot use the
old point-delay CDF. The distributed-delay race CDF is

\[
C_T(t)
=
c_A(t)+\bar c_E(t)-c_A(t)\bar c_E(t)
=
1-[1-c_A(t)][1-\bar c_E(t)].
\]

For stimulus-relative RT limits, the likelihood denominator is therefore

\[
C_T(t_{\mathrm{stim}}+U)-C_T(t_{\mathrm{stim}}+L).
\]

### Simulation and implementation checks

1. Draw one delay \(D\) per simulated trial and use that same value throughout
   the trial's evidence trajectory.
2. Verify that the limit \(b-a\rightarrow 0\) recovers the existing point-delay
   likelihood.
3. Compare the analytic uniform-delay equations with numerical integration near
   evidence onset, where finite spectral series are most sensitive.
4. The current \(K=100\) antiderivative curve is a useful feasibility result,
   but numerical integration remains the reference check before using the
   analytic expression in an SVI fit.

## Uniform-delay simulator versus analytic likelihood

![Three posterior-derived proactive/reactive parameter sets crossed with three uniform evidence-delay distributions. Simulated choice-positive joint RT densities are plotted above zero and choice-negative densities are mirrored below zero; blue curves are the analytic K=100 uniform-delay likelihood.](../assets/results/2026-08-05/uniform_delay_simulator_vs_analytic_likelihood_3x3.png)

*Full-distribution comparison for three complete posterior-derived parameter sets and Uniform(77.5, 82.5), Uniform(70, 90), and Uniform(30, 130) ms evidence delays. Each cell uses 100,000 simulations with one delay draw per trial. Negative stimulus-relative RTs are retained, and neither choice density is truncated or renormalized: the positive and absolute negative areas therefore equal the two choice fractions and sum to one. The display is cropped to -1 to 1 s only after those full-support calculations. Simulation steps are 0.25 ms for the near-median case, 0.10 ms for the slow-evidence case, and 0.05 ms for the fast-evidence case to control scalar Euler boundary-crossing bias.*

All nine cells passed the predeclared checks. Analytic total mass was
\(0.999999\text{--}1.000000\); the largest simulation-versus-analytic sub-CDF
difference was \(0.00968\); and the largest analytic-versus-independent 64-node
Gauss-Legendre sub-CDF difference was \(9.4\times10^{-7}\). The tiny negative
analytic density minimum in the fastest case was numerical roundoff
\(-1.1\times10^{-12}\).

The experimental utility evaluates the inverse-Gaussian proactive CDF with
`log_ndtr`. This is algebraically equivalent to the existing expression but
avoids an extreme-tail underflow found for the LED34/57 parameter set; leaving
that underflow in place made the proactive PDF and CDF numerically inconsistent.
The production point-delay likelihood was not modified.
